Personal use and operating limits

Version 1.1 · Updated 25 September 2026

Private Recorder is a personal Android microphone recorder with optional backups to accounts you authorize. This page explains practical use and limits; it does not establish whether a particular recording or use of a recording is permitted.

Start and stop deliberately

Start recording from the app and grant the requested microphone and notification permissions. Android’s microphone indicator and recording notification remain visible. The app does not disguise itself, hide Android’s recording indicators, or start microphone capture merely because it was installed.

Use the Stop control when you want to finish recording. After a reboot, force-stop, or a recording failure that cannot be recovered, reopen the app and explicitly start again. The app is a microphone recorder, not a telephone-call recorder.

Check the recordings you need

No promise of uninterrupted 24/7 recording or immediate cloud delivery is made. Test the actual phone and inspect playable audio before depending on the setup.

Calls, competing microphone apps, Android or manufacturer battery controls, device heat, power loss, full storage, and network problems can interrupt capture or delay backup. A growing file or a matching hash does not establish that useful sound was recorded.

File hashes and timestamps help compare saved bytes and understand the recording timeline. They do not certify who spoke, accurate real-world times, complete capture, authenticity, or legal admissibility. For a consequential use, obtain the appropriate professional guidance for your circumstances.

Manage storage and data usage

Original recordings remain on the phone after cloud backup. Monitor phone storage and each cloud account’s quota. Uninstalling the app or clearing its data removes the private phone archive, so preserve needed copies before maintenance or updates.

Cloud backup begins after you connect each provider. The default network policy permits Wi-Fi or mobile data. The optional unmetered-network setting follows Android’s network classification. Verification downloads uploaded files again, so backups consume both upload and download data.

Control account access and copies

Connect only accounts you intend to use. Review both providers separately when using two cloud destinations. Disconnecting stops future scheduled backups but keeps existing local and cloud files. Manage cloud deletions, sharing, and account authorization through the corresponding provider’s controls.

A verified-transfer receipt is historical. A cloud copy may later be deleted or modified without being detected by the app. Keep independent copies of important recordings and periodically verify that you can open and play them.

Read the privacy information for details about account permissions, stored sign-in state, and encryption. Use recording and sharing features only where you have the necessary permission under the rules that apply to you.
